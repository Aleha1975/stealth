A non-custodial way to receive stablecoins on one-time addresses and to move them out again: directly, through one swap, or through the Zcash shielded pool and back, from a wallet that exists only for that transfer.

This document describes how the system is built, what each route costs and how long it takes on real money, what an outside observer can and cannot see, how a stuck transfer is recovered from keys alone, and what is still open. It is written for the owner and for readers who want to understand the system before they rely on it.

STL-BP-001 · Rev CSection 1 · Summary

1Summary

1.1Purpose and scope

Stealth is a non-custodial way to receive stablecoins on one-time addresses and to move them out again. A payer sends to a fresh address derived from the receiver's published link. Only the receiver's keys can find it. Those keys come from one wallet signature and never leave the browser. The pay contract holds no balance, has no owner and cannot be upgraded.

Moving the money out is where privacy is won or lost, so Stealth offers three routes. Standard is a direct, gasless transfer. Private fast puts one swap between the two addresses. Maximum privacy sends the money through the Zcash shielded pool and back, paid out in two parts, from a throw-away wallet that the page builds and drives by itself.

This document describes how each route works, what it costs and how long it takes on real money, what an outside observer can and cannot see, how a stuck or abandoned transfer is recovered from keys alone, and which gaps are still open. Where a figure is not obvious it carries a tag.

2.3Choosing a route

Maximum privacy has a high fixed cost, because Zcash fees do not shrink with the amount. At 10 USDG a dry quote already shows 11.75% with two parts, and the live run lost more (Section 8). With two parts a dry quote falls under 3% from about 47 USDG. Below that, Private fast is usually cheaper. If hiding the link from an on-chain reader is not the aim, Standard costs the least.

STL-BP-001 · Rev CSection 3 · End-to-end flows

3End-to-end flows

Each route as built. Arrows follow the order of events; boxes with an accent outline run in the browser or at the swap venue, tinted boxes are Zcash steps.

3.1Standard

The stealth key signs an EIP-3009 authorization. The relayer checks it, pays the gas and submits it. The token moves from the stealth address to the recipient in one transaction. The page waits for the receipt and checks the transfer in its log.

3.2Private fast

The amount is split into one or two random parts when it is at least 5 tokens. Each part waits a random time of up to 3 minutes, takes its own quote with the refund address set to the stealth address itself, and is deposited through the relayer. Parts go strictly one after the other.

3.3Maximum privacy

The seven states of the hop run in the browser and are saved after every step. The first four bring the money to the shielded pool. The last three pay it out in two parts, each with its own quote, its own deposit and its own refund address. A refunded part is shielded again and tried once more.

STL-BP-001 · Rev CSection 4 · What an outside observer sees

4What an outside observer sees

Stealth does not make a transfer untraceable. It removes the direct on-chain link between the one-time address and the recipient, and it makes a watcher match by sum and time window instead of reading a transfer. This section says what is readable at each hop of the maximum route, and who can connect which pieces.

4.1Hop by hop

4.2What links the two ends

4.2.1
The swap venue. It takes the quote for each leg and its public status lists the recipient of every deposit address. The two legs share only protocol constants: no referral or user field, a quote deadline of one hour plus or minus ten random minutes, no cookies and no referrer. The same network address and browser still reach it unless the second leg is sent over another path.
4.2.2
The lightwalletd proxy. It sees the network address, the block ranges the wallet asks for and the transactions it broadcasts. It cannot spend. A lying proxy could report wrong balances or hold back a transaction. It cannot redirect funds.
4.2.3
The relayer. It sees the stealth address and the deposit address of the first leg. It can delay or refuse a request. It cannot change the recipient or the amount, because both are signed.
4.2.4
The browser. The stealth key and the hop mnemonic live in page memory. A page-injection flaw would read them, so the page sets a strict content-security policy (Section 10).

4.3The limit

The limit, plainly. The split into two parts defeats a match on one amount only. The two parts sum to the amount that went in minus known fees, both go to the same recipient, and the pipeline latency is close to constant. An observer who can see both ends can still match on the sum and on the time window, and the fewer people use the route at the same time, the easier that is. The random pause adds noise to the timing. It does not hide it. (This is the comment on MODES in the hop module.)

Private fast has a weaker form of the same limit: the link is broken by the swap, not by cryptography. Standard has no break at all, and says so on the screen.

5.2Halts, retries and resume

5.2.1
Halt is a deliberate stop from a safety check. Nothing retries by itself. The card says what stopped and the person presses Continue. The money is where the state says it is: on the stealth address, in the hop wallet, or in the swap.
5.2.2
Transient errors (network, RPC, quote service) retry four times inside the step, waiting 2 s, 6 s and 18 s. After that the app resumes the hop on its own at 15 s, 30 s, 1, 2, 4, 5 and 5 min, seven times at most, then waits for Continue configured.
5.2.3
Order of writes. The signed authorization is saved before it can leave the device. The raw Zcash transaction and the wallet bytes are saved before the broadcast. A marker that a shield may have gone out blocks a second shield for 15 minutes.
5.2.4
One tab at a time. A browser lock keeps a second tab from driving the same address, and a screen wake lock is requested because a sleeping phone freezes the tab.
5.2.5
Progress is real progress. The percentage on the card counts finished steps and facts the machine has seen (confirmations, the payout part, the pause timer). It never advances on elapsed time alone.
5.2.6
Cleanup. A finished transfer is scrubbed to what the next hop needs: no recipient, no wallet bytes stay behind.
STL-BP-001 · Rev CSection 6 · Pauses in depth

6Pauses in depth

A pause is the part of the route that costs time on purpose. This section explains why it exists, which values are set now, and how it interacts with the fact that the two payouts run strictly one after the other. The values changed on 31 Oct 2026, set by the owner.

6.1Why pauses exist

Without a pause, the interval between the deposit and the payout is just the pipeline's own latency, and that is close to the same every time. A constant interval is a fingerprint. An observer who sees a deposit can look for a payout that lands a constant time later. The pause draws part of that interval at random, so the interval varies from one transfer to the next. It is timing noise, not cryptography, and it only works against an observer who matches on time.

6.2Current values

The older modes stay in the code only so that a saved hop of those modes resumes with its own plan. fast was 1–2 parts with the last send 3..10 min after the shield and private was 2–3 parts with 8..15 min. Neither is offered.

6.3How a plan is drawn

6.3.1
The last send time is drawn at random within the range (2..6 min now).
6.3.2
The other send times split that total by random weights between 0.6 and 1.4.
6.3.3
The first send is then capped at firstMax, so the last part is not queued behind a first part that was planned late.
6.3.4
The two part sizes are random, each at least 150,000 zatoshi, all different, none a round multiple of 0.0001 ZEC, and each part keeps its own fee reserve.

6.4Interaction with strictly sequential payouts

A part is sent at its planned time or when the previous part has ended, whichever is later. A send takes about 0.6 min to quote and 8 min to send configured, 8.6 min in all computed. The first part ends at its start plus 8.6 min, so the second part's planned time (at most 6 min now) is always earlier than that end. In practice the second payout follows the first as soon as it has ended. The gap between the two payouts is the send duration of the first, not a random draw. The screen says so.

That is also what the run showed. In run B, with the previous settings, the pause before payout 1 was 331 s and the pause before payout 2 was 45 s measured.

6.5Old against new

Planned start windows follow from the plan rules computed. Bars show the worst case, with the first part starting at the latest time its window allows. In that case the last payout ends 4.0 minutes sooner (23.2 against 19.2) computed.

6.6The trade-off

Table 6.2 Shorter pauses against longer pauses

11The relayer

The relayer exists so that a one-time address never needs ETH for gas. The stealth key signs a transfer authorization in the browser. The relayer pays the gas from its own account and sends it. One service runs per chain, with its own state and log.

11.1Overview

11.2Two rules

11.2.1
It sends only what the stealth key signed, once. The authorization names the recipient, the amount, the validity window and a random nonce. The relayer cannot change any of them. It may be slow or refuse; it cannot redirect. An authorization is used on chain once, so a replay does nothing.
11.2.2
It serves only addresses that were paid through Stealth, and only up to what they hold. The indexer must know the address as paid through the pay contract, and the balance must cover the amount. Arbitrary addresses cannot spend the relayer's gas.

11.3Nonce handling

11.3.1
The local nonce is reconciled with the node's pending count every time the lock is taken. One sender runs at a time.
11.3.2
A send in flight is tracked until it settles. If it never reached the node, the next holder of the lock finds a gap (nothing on the wire, no recent send of ours at that nonce, two agreeing reads 4 s apart, 15 s of grace) and fills it with a self-transfer. No restart is needed.
11.3.3
Nonce reads, transaction probes and sends all go to one sticky RPC endpoint, the first in the list. It moves to the next only when the endpoint is unusable, and the switch is logged.
11.3.4
A record that a send may be going out is written to disk and flushed before the send. If the state cannot be saved the relayer answers 503 and sends nothing. If the state file exists but cannot be read, the relayer refuses to start.
11.3.5
A stuck transaction is replaced once at the same nonce with a higher fee: after 20 s on Robinhood Chain (60 s by default), +30% configured.

STL-BP-001 · Rev CSection 12 · Loss cap and approval

12Loss cap and approval

12.1Purpose

A private hop can lose a lot on a small amount, and the amount in play changes while it runs. The loss cap makes sure nobody pays out at a rate worse than the one they agreed to without being asked.

12.2The rule

12.2.1
Projected loss of the whole transfer = 1 − (value paid out so far + this quote's rate × the ZEC still to send) ÷ the amount that left the stealth address. It is computed at every payout quote.
12.2.2
The cap is 3% configured. A quote above it passes silently when its projected loss stays within the figure the person approved plus QUOTE_TOL, 2 points (0.02) configured.
12.2.3
The approval is the click. Pressing Confirm on the preview accepts the preview's total cost, stored as approvedLoss. The preview comes from two dry quotes, so what is approved is the fee share the person saw.
12.2.4
Beyond the approval, a run that the person just started or continued by clicking opens a review dialog. A run driven by a timer (a pause that ended, an automatic retry) never opens a modal: it halts with the ZEC still shielded and asks the person to press Continue transfer.

12.4Timer against click

12.4.1
Run A paused for about 36 hours on the old loss cap measured. The quote's projected loss was above 3% and the run waited for a person. Since then the Confirm click carries the approval, so a transfer the person already accepted at its preview cost goes through, and a transfer whose rate has moved by more than the tolerance stops and asks.
12.4.2
A rebuilt transfer (found from the key alone) has no original amount to compare with. Its payout is shown to the person against what was found on chain and needs a click. If a later quote is worse than the approved rate by more than the tolerance, it asks again.
12.4.3
A dialog can still open from an unattended stage of a click-started run (the permission survives the pause). The latest review rates that Low (Section 17).
STL-BP-001 · Rev CSection 13 · Recovery

13Recovery

A hop can be interrupted in many ways: a tab closed, a browser cleared, a phone lost. Every path back starts from the same thing, the wallet signature. The hop wallet is a pure function of the stealth key, so the addresses that can hold ZEC can be rebuilt without any saved record.

13.1Recovery paths

13.2The finder

13.2.1
For each payment the page counts how many hops that stealth address has started (the number of authorizations the token has recorded for it). It then derives the hop wallets below that count, newest first, at most 8 of them, and 4 when the count cannot be read. A hop wallet that has a local record is left to that record. For every other wallet it asks the proxy for the unspent outputs of every account and 3 address indexes, one cheap call each, 150 ms apart so a page of payments does not hammer the proxy. The wallet with the most ZEC is shown.

13.3The age rule

13.3.1
A found output must be at least 100 blocks old (about 2 h at 75 s per block) configured before a lost-record card may act on it. A hop that is still running on another device finishes well inside that window, so the age rule keeps two devices from working on the same coins. An unconfirmed output, an unknown tip or an unknown height all count as young.

13.4Return and Continue

13.4.1
Return money works from the keys alone. It derives the transparent keys of the hop wallet (path m/44'/133'/account'/0/index), builds a transparent-only transaction (ZIP 225 and 244, the NU6.3 branch id), signs it in the page and sends it through the proxy. It then takes a quote for the ZEC into the stablecoin, refunds to the hop address and pays out to the person's wallet. After the broadcast it asks the proxy to hand back its own raw bytes before it calls the sweep sent.
13.4.2
Continue is offered only when all the ZEC sits on account 0, index 0. It writes a new hop record (marked as rebuilt) at the SHIELD step and the machine carries on. A rebuilt hop has no original amount, so its payout needs a click (Section 12).
13.4.3
Both write an intent record before anything is broadcast, under the same lock that the hop machine uses. A later press looks at the earlier intent first: if its transaction is known it follows the swap, if it is provably dead it lets it expire, and if the page cannot tell it stops. Nothing is swept twice.

13.5Tested live

0.00464296 ZEC was found from the key alone in an empty browser, and +5.694839 USDG arrived in about 5 minutes measured. The swap lost about 2.7% measured.

This test covered the transparent side, which is where the money waits before it is shielded and where a refunded payout part lands. ZEC that is already inside the shielded pool when a record is lost needs the wallet to be rebuilt from the key and rescanned from its birthday. That path was not part of the live test (Section 17).

Nothing is stored that cannot be rebuilt, and nothing that must be rebuilt depends on a server. The chain below turns one wallet signature into every key the page needs.

14.1Derivation chain

15.2Planned service fee

Planned. The service fee is intended to be 0.30% (30 bps) on both private routes once it is enabled. It is not enabled. It needs the NEAR account that receives it, which is not set. Until then the figures on Sections 7 and 8 contain no service fee of ours.

Why the fee sits on the first leg: a fee field on both legs would be a shared value that ties them together. With it on the first leg only, the second leg's quote request is unchanged.

16.2What the pay contract does

16.2.1
One token per deployment, set in the constructor and immutable. payToken and payWithAuthorization revert for any other token, and payETH pays native ETH.
16.2.2
It moves the tokens straight from the payer to the one-time address and publishes the ERC-5564 announcement in the same transaction. It keeps no balance. There is no owner, no upgrade and no protocol fee.
16.2.3
It checks that the one-time address received exactly the announced amount, so fee-on-transfer and lying tokens are refused.
16.2.4
A one-time address can be paid once through the contract (the used flag), so one address never links two payers to one receiver. An address with code is refused, and so are addresses in the precompile range below 0x10000.
16.2.5
payWithAuthorization takes one payer signature (EIP-3009 receive authorization) so the payer needs no gas.
16.2.6
A known residual: a watcher could pay a pending one-time address one unit first and make the real payment revert. The payer loses only gas and retries with a new address. On Base and Robinhood Chain the sequencer is private, so this needs its cooperation. Accepted.
